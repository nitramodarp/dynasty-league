# 40s and Blunts Weekly Rolling Coverage
### Week 15 · The one where the commissioner became the matchup of the week

---

**MATCHUP OF THE WEEK**

Yahoo, in its institutional wisdom, designated Bad MoFo's over Chief Noc-A-Homa, 8–2 as the matchup of the week. This is the commissioner's team. The commissioner lost eight categories. He held SB and SV+H, which is another way of saying he won the two categories that cannot carry you home. Bad MoFo's took R, HR, RBI, OPS, K, ERA, WHIP, and QS — a list that covers offense, pitching, and most of what the game of baseball contains. The league has reviewed this result and can confirm it is accurate.

---

**LAST WEEK'S RESULTS**

**Bad MoFo's def. Chief Noc-A-Homa, 8–2.** Bad MoFo's arrived, apparently, and they brought starting pitching. ERA, WHIP, and QS all went their way, which is not the natural order of things — the commissioner has built his entire operation on owning exactly those categories. Chief Noc-A-Homa salvaged SB and SV+H. Third place in the standings, for what it's worth.

**Bell Biv Devo def. Section 34, 7–3.** Bell Biv Devo swept the ratios and added R, RBI, SB, and QS for good measure. Section 34 responded with HR, K, and SV+H, which is a reasonable consolation prize and also a complete description of what they had to offer. A 7–3 week for Bell Biv Devo, who appear to be doing something.

**L-A-Loosh def. Bip Roberts, 7–3.** There it is. The sleeping bear has been awake since June and apparently nobody told Bip Roberts. L-A-Loosh took R, OPS, K, ERA, WHIP, QS, and SV+H. Bip answered with HR, RBI, and SB, which on most weeks would represent a competent loss. This is the part of the season where ranking L-A-Loosh ninth starts to feel irresponsible.

**Stone Jack Ballers def. Grand Theft Votto, 9–1.** Grand Theft Votto won SB. That is the entire list of things Grand Theft Votto won. Stone Jack Ballers took the other nine categories with the calm, methodical energy of a person who transacted aggressively all week and expected results. The system worked.

**MayTheSchwarBeWithU def. The Chicago Way, 9–1.** The Chicago Way won SB. Both 9–1 results on the same week, with both losers salvaging only stolen bases, is either a coincidence or a commentary on stolen bases as a category. The Chicago Way, who entered the week as a quiet, slightly suspicious contender, dropped a game that will require some quiet, suspicious explanation.

**Thurgood Jenkins def. Old Fashioned, 8–2.** Old Fashioned took SB and QS, which means they had pitchers starting games and baserunners in motion, and yet the scoreline was 8–2. This happens when you are Old Fashioned.

**QUESTIONABLE ACTIVITY**

The week began at 4:46 in the morning on Saturday, June 28th, when Bip Roberts dropped Bryson Stott to add Nick Pivetta. Bip Roberts then added Stott back on July 1st and dropped him again on July 5th in favor of Kody Clemens. This is not a transaction strategy so much as a Bryson Stott policy that has not yet resolved.

Bad MoFo's opened Saturday by adding Brady Singer and Michael King, dropping Ranger Suarez and Gregory Soto to make room. Singer was tagged as a streamer and was dropped the following morning. The King addition stuck. The week went 8–2, so the overall approach appears defensible.

The Chicago Way made four moves in eleven minutes on Saturday — dropping Ezequiel Duran and Seranthony Domínguez, then adding Hunter Gaddis and Luis García Jr. This is either a decisive roster reset or what happens when someone finally has a free Saturday morning. Then, on Wednesday, they dropped Teoscar Hernández. Teoscar Hernández was on this roster. This information is provided without further comment.

Grand Theft Votto added Mitch Keller on Saturday, dropped him for Peter Lambert on Sunday, dropped Lambert for Noah Schultz on Tuesday, and arrived back at approximately where they started. The round trip was completed in four days.

Stone Jack Ballers operated on a different cadence entirely. Between Sunday afternoon and early Friday morning, the roster turned over at a pace that suggests either deep conviction or a very fast internet connection. Among the more notable movements: Jake McCarthy was added Sunday, dropped Monday night, and re-added Friday night. Zebby Matthews came and went in two days. Michael Wacha arrived Monday, departed Wednesday. The net result was a 9–1 win, so the approach carries.

L-A-Loosh added Elvis Alvarado on Monday afternoon, dropped him fourteen minutes later for Yohandy Morales, re-added Alvarado the following Tuesday morning, and dropped him again eight minutes after that for Tyron Guerrero. Elvis Alvarado did not play for L-A-Loosh in any meaningful sense. He was a placeholder between thoughts.

MayTheSchwarBeWithU dropped Spencer Strider and added Jake Bennett, which is the kind of move you make when you have decided the season requires a different plan. They also dropped Jason Adam for TJ Rumfield on Thursday. The week ended 9–1, so whatever the plan was, it worked for the week.

Section 34 dropped Walker Buehler on Wednesday and then cycled through Slade Cecconi and Trevor Rogers as replacements over the next two days. The audition process is ongoing.

Old Fashioned made no moves. This is no longer worth questioning.
