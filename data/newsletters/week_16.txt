MATCHUP OF THE WEEK

Stone Jack Ballers def. Thurgood Jenkins, 9–0–1. Yahoo designated this the Matchup of the Week, which is a reasonable institutional choice given that a 9–0–1 result is, in this league's currency, a near-perfect game. Stone Jack took nine categories outright — runs, home runs, RBI, stolen bases, OPS, strikeouts, ERA, WHIP, saves-plus-holds — and split QS with a tie. Thurgood Jenkins won zero categories. That is not a misprint. You can go look it up. This was not a competitive fantasy baseball contest; it was a calendar week that happened to Thurgood Jenkins.

LAST WEEK'S RESULTS

Chief Noc-A-Homa def. Old Fashioned, 8–2. Chief took R, HR, RBI, SB, OPS, strikeouts, WHIP, and saves-plus-holds. Old Fashioned, in one of its more spirited performances in recent memory, claimed ERA and QS. The league acknowledges Old Fashioned's pitching was genuinely better this week and has no additional comment on the subject.

Bad MoFo's def. MayTheSchwarBeWithU, 6–2–2. Bad MoFo's won RBI, SB, OPS, ERA, WHIP, and QS. MayTheSchwarBeWithU took runs and saves-plus-holds, and the two sides agreed on a draw in home runs and strikeouts. A 6–2–2 line from Bad MoFo's is not a fluke; it is a team playing well enough to sit third in the league, which is a sentence that requires a moment to sit in.

Grand Theft Votto def. Bell Biv Devo, 7–2–1. Grand Theft Votto swept the offense — runs, home runs, RBI, stolen bases, OPS — and added strikeouts and QS for good measure. Bell Biv Devo held ERA and WHIP, and the two tied in saves-plus-holds. Bell Biv Devo won the pitching ratios and the moral satisfaction of holding a lead that the box score did not reflect. Grand Theft Votto won everything else.

Bip Roberts def. Section 34, 7–2–1. Bip took runs, RBI, stolen bases, strikeouts, ERA, WHIP, and QS. Section 34 answered with home runs and OPS, and the saves-plus-holds column settled into a tie. Section 34 winning OPS while losing the matchup 7–2 is a very specific kind of bad luck, the kind where you hit the ball hard and lose anyway.

L-A-Loosh def. The Chicago Way, 7–3. The Chicago Way took home runs, strikeouts, and saves-plus-holds. L-A-Loosh took everything else — runs, RBI, stolen bases, OPS, ERA, WHIP, and QS. This is the kind of result that, if you told someone in April, would prompt a shrug. It is now mid-July, L-A-Loosh is in the playoffs by 1.5 wins, and the shrug has curdled slightly.

PLAYOFF PICTURE

Six teams are in; six teams are not. The top four — Chief Noc-A-Homa, Stone Jack Ballers, Bad MoFo's, and Bip Roberts — are each clear of the cut line by enough that the math stops being interesting. The Chicago Way, in fifth, holds 6.5 wins of breathing room. L-A-Loosh, in sixth, has 1.5.

That 1.5 is the number that matters. Bell Biv Devo and Section 34 are each 1.5 back, sitting in identical positions below the line with six weeks left and roughly 60 category decisions still on the table. Grand Theft Votto is 3.5 back, Thurgood Jenkins is four back, and MayTheSchwarBeWithU is 4.5 back — all technically alive, none of them comfortable. Old Fashioned is 20 back of the sixth seed. This is not a conversation about Old Fashioned.

The trade deadline is Thursday, August 20. No further guidance is provided here.

QUESTIONABLE ACTIVITY

MayTheSchwarBeWithU opened the week by dropping Samad Taylor, then three hours later added Jake Bauers and dropped Josh Naylor. By Saturday they had also dropped Kyle Teel and added Kyle Karros. Three roster moves, three different players who will not be on this team in two weeks. The auditions continue.

Bip Roberts added Kody Clemens on July 6th and dropped him on July 10th. Four days. Whatever Kody Clemens was supposed to provide, it was apparently a four-day quantity of it.

Bell Biv Devo made three separate adds on July 6th — Ike Irish at 7:52 AM, Cole Carrigg at 7:56 AM, and Reynaldo López at 2:34 PM. Then on July 9th, dropped JoJo Romero. The morning of the 6th reads like a shopping cart left open overnight.

Stone Jack Ballers added Michael Wacha at 3:11 AM on July 6th — a timestamp that raises no specific concerns and yet raises some concerns — then pivoted to Teoscar Hernández, then Taj Bradley, then Tommy Edman for a spot start. Stone Jack transacted six times this week and went 9–0–1. This information is presented without editorial position.

Chief Noc-A-Homa dropped Sam Bachman and Ryan Helsley within two minutes of each other on Tuesday morning, then added A.J. Ewing, then dropped A.J. Ewing two days later. Helsley was dropped having previously been saved as a holds asset; Ewing lasted 48 hours. The commissioner's roster is in a state of active revision.

Thurgood Jenkins added Luis Arraez and dropped Max Muncy on the same morning, which is a philosophical reorientation — trading power and eligibility for contact and batting average. Arraez hit .354 last year. Muncy hit the ball hard. The league does not arbitrate these decisions; it simply notes them.

L-A-Loosh dropped Nico Hoerner on Saturday. Hoerner had been a fine piece. There was no announcement.

The Moneyball Award — L-A-Loosh: 30.8 wins per $100M ($234M payroll, 72 wins). L-A-Loosh is spending the least meaningful money in the league and sitting in the playoffs. This is either very good roster construction or a coincidence that has gone on long enough to become indistinguishable from strategy.

The Salary-Cap Sucker — Stone Jack Ballers: 19.4 wins per $100M ($464M payroll, 90 wins). Nearly half a billion dollars in salary, 90 wins, a 9–0–1 week, and three risers in the ticker. The efficiency number is bad. Everything else about this week was not.

Dead Money — Chief Noc-A-Homa: $66M of salary on the IL. The commissioner is carrying $66 million in players who are not playing baseball. This is not a knock on the commissioner so much as it is a fact about the commissioner.
