# 40s and Blunts Weekly Rolling Coverage

### Week 22 · The last week of the regular season

---

Nineteen weeks in first place, and Chief Noc-A-Homa didn't get to close the
regular season on top. Stone Jack Ballers held on by a single game — .586 to
.582 — while both teams lost their final matchups on the same Sunday. Loosh
put it best a few weeks ago: regular season championships are cute. The
commissioner's office would like the record to reflect: cute *af*. $48 already
changed hands this week — two division titles and the Regular Season
Championship, settled. What's left is the real money.

---

**MATCHUP OF THE WEEK**

**The Chicago Way 6, Chief Noc-A-Homa 4.** Chicago took home runs, RBI, steals,
ERA, WHIP, and saves-plus-holds; Chief salvaged runs, OPS, strikeouts, and
quality starts. It didn't cost Chief the #2 seed — Loosh was never close
enough to matter — but the #2 overall seed still lost its finale to the
fifth-place team. Chicago locks in as the 5-seed. Chief locks in as the #2
overall seed and a first-round bye.

---

**LAST WEEK'S RESULTS**

**Bell Biv Devo 7, Old Fashioned 3.** Bell Biv Devo closes the regular season
the same way it's spent the last six weeks — beating up on somebody. Seeds
into the Toilet Bowl at #9, drawing Old Fashioned again in the opening round.

**Bip Roberts 7, Bad MoFo's 3.** The bounce-back from the 10-0 sweep was real —
Bip took seven categories clean. It wasn't enough. Bad MoFo's held the 6-seed
by a game and a half anyway. Bip settles for the 7-seed and a bye into the
Toilet Bowl semifinal — the other bracket entirely.

**MayTheSchwarBeWithU 5, Thurgood Jenkins 4, one tie.** Fourth straight week
this team has shown up in this newsletter for one reason or another. Seeds
into the Toilet Bowl at #10, and draws Thurgood again in Week 23.

**Grand Theft Votto 1, L-A-Loosh 6, three ties.** This was the game. Three
excellent weeks in a row, needing one more, and Votto lost six of seven
decided categories in the one week that had to go right. Misses the playoff
field by 4.5 games. Enters the Toilet Bowl as the 8-seed with a first-round
bye — no consolation for a team that needed this exact win three days ago and
didn't get it.

**Stone Jack Ballers 3, Section 34 6, one tie.** SJ backed into the overall #1
seed anyway — losing the final game of the regular season and still finishing
a game ahead of Chief, who also lost. Section 34 locks in as the 4-seed off
the upset.

**REGULAR SEASON CHAMPION — SETTLED**

Stone Jack Ballers, .586 to .582, by a single game, decided while both teams
were losing. $12, and bragging rights that last exactly as long as it takes
someone to point out both division titles were never actually in question —
Chief ran away with Colt 45, SJ ran away with Olde English, months ago. Cute,
like the man said. Moving on.

**POWER RANKINGS — WHO'S HOT, WHO'S NOT**

Seeding is built on 22 weeks. This is built on three — Weeks 20 through 22,
the only stretch that tells you anything about who's actually playing well
right now. Championship bracket only.

1. **Section 34** (19-9-2, .667) — Hottest team in the bracket, and it's the
reason this team is a 4-seed instead of a 5. The tiebreaker rewarded exactly
what these numbers show.
2. **L-A-Loosh** (17-8-5, .650) — Second-hottest, and it drew Bad MoFo's — the
second-coldest — in the opening round. Bad matchup for whoever's on the other
side of that.
3. **The Chicago Way** (15-14-1, .517) — Right at .500 across the stretch,
but the shape is what matters: buried at 1-9 in Week 20, elite at 8-1-1 in
Week 21. Trending the right direction into a 4-vs-5 matchup with Section 34.
3. **Stone Jack Ballers** (15-14-1, .517) — Tied with Chicago, dead even, and
still walks in with the #1 overall seed and a bye. The Regular Season
Championship was won on the strength of one outlier week, not three.
5. **Bad MoFo's** (12-17-1, .417) — The 6-seed, and the second-coldest team
in the bracket heading into it. Draws the second-hottest team in the first
round.
6. **Chief Noc-A-Homa** (10-19-1, .350) — The #2 overall seed has been the
coldest team in the Championship bracket for three straight weeks. The bye
buys time. It doesn't fix whatever this is.

**ON THE WIRE**

**Chicago armed both of its rivals without meaning to.** On the morning of
August 24th, in a ten-minute stretch, Chicago dropped both Oneil Cruz and Jazz
Chisholm Jr. to the waiver wire. Three days later, at 4:20 a.m., Chief claimed
Cruz over Bip and Loosh, and Stone Jack Ballers claimed Chisholm over Chief
and Bip — the two teams that just finished 1-2 in the league, both picking up
pieces Chicago decided it didn't need.

**Stone Jack Ballers ran a three-transaction, sixty-second catcher swap at
3:52 p.m. on the final Sunday of the regular season** — Sean Murphy out for
Chris Bassitt, immediately reversed, immediately reversed again — while
clinging to a one-game lead for the Regular Season Championship. Whatever it
was chasing, it apparently found it with seven minutes to spare.
