# 40s and Blunts Weekly Rolling Coverage

### Week 19 · The one where the sixth seed changed hands again

---

**MATCHUP OF THE WEEK**

Yahoo flagged **Chief Noc-A-Homa vs. L-A-Loosh** a week in advance, and for the
second straight Monday the algorithm looks smarter than it has any business
looking. Loosh came in having gone 7–2–1 and 7–3 in his previous two weeks. He
made it three good weeks in a row and lost anyway, 6–4.

The hitting was not close, and it went the wrong way. L-A-Loosh took runs 31–24,
home runs 8–4, RBI 25–17, and OPS .706 to .633 — four offensive categories,
every one of them clean, against the team that leads the league. In most weeks
that is the ballgame.

It was not the ballgame because Loosh threw 39 innings and Chief threw 51.2.
Twelve and two-thirds extra innings turned into strikeouts, 56–44. They turned
into quality starts, 5–3. They did not hurt the ratios, which finished 2.44 to
3.92 and 0.95 to 1.26. Saves-plus-holds went 6–4. Six stolen bases to three
closed the door on a week where Loosh out-hit his opponent in four of five
offensive columns.

L-A-Loosh has made seventy-three roster moves this season, more than every team
but two, and has climbed from ninth in June to sixth in August. He is now 19.5
back, tied on percentage with the team directly ahead of him, and holding the
last playoff spot by a tiebreaker. It's been one long Truckin'. It has just
stopped being fun.

**LAST WEEK'S RESULTS**

**Chief Noc-A-Homa def. L-A-Loosh, 6–4.** See above.

**SJ Ballers def. Bad MoFo's, 7–3.** SJ Ballers won this with the bats and very
nearly gave it back on the mound. The at-bat totals were 224 to 223, as close to
identical as the format allows, and SJ hit .286 against .256 and posted a .826
OPS against .721. Runs 39–33, home runs 11–5, RBI 33–29. Four hitting categories,
none of them close, none of them attributable to anything but better hitting.

The pitching went 3–2 SJ and did not deserve to. Fifty-five innings against 44.2
bought strikeouts 64–46 and quality starts 4–2, which is what ten extra innings
buy. Those same innings produced a 4.75 ERA and a 1.45 WHIP against Bad MoFo's
4.23 and 1.30. Saves-plus-holds went 4–3. Bad MoFo's pitched better, lost the
pitching week three categories to two, and lost the matchup somewhere else
entirely.

On Saturday night, with a day left to play, SJ Ballers posted a trading-block
notice reading "K, ERA, WHIP Wanted." He won strikeouts and lost both ratios. The
self-diagnosis was two-thirds correct and arrived six days into a seven-day week.

**Thurgood Jenkins def. Section 34, 6–3–1.** Thurgood posted a .875 OPS, eleven
home runs, and 35 RBI and still needed a decimal place to finish it. Both teams
show a WHIP of 1.23 — a Divided Sky before the tiebreaker split it — and the
category went to Thurgood on the numbers behind the rounding. Quality starts
finished tied at one apiece, which is its own kind of
statement. Section 34 answered with 32 runs, three stolen bases, and nine
saves-plus-holds, and hit .613 as a team, a combination that is difficult to
assemble even on purpose. This result decided the sixth seed; see the playoff
picture.

**The Chicago Way def. Bell Biv Devo, 6–1–3.** Bell Biv Devo won quality starts
5–4 and tied stolen bases and strikeouts, 3-3 and 54-54. Everything else went to
Chicago: runs 33–23, home runs 15–5, RBI 31–19, OPS .873 to .634, ERA 3.43 to
3.50, WHIP 1.02 to 1.20, and saves-plus-holds 6–1. Fifteen home runs in a week is
not a hot streak, it is a policy decision, and Chicago has been quietly making it
for a month.

**Bip Roberts def. MayTheSchwarBeWithU, 7–2–1.** Bip won seven categories, tied
quality starts at three, and lost OPS by four thousandths of a point, .853 to
.849 — the sort of margin that sends a manager back through the box score
looking for a specific Thursday. Jordan's two points were that and seven stolen
bases. Bip's 2.52 ERA, 1.10 WHIP, and eight saves-plus-holds handled the rest.
MayTheSchwarBeWithU made ten roster moves during the week and won two
categories. The league office has logged the ratio and offers nothing further.

**Grand Theft Votto def. Old Fashioned, 8–2.** Old Fashioned made zero moves,
won ERA at 3.00 and WHIP at 1.24, and lost the other eight. Votto took 36 runs,
ten home runs, 31 RBI, six steals, a .740 OPS, 57 strikeouts, quality starts,
and saves-plus-holds. Grand Theft Votto has now gone 8–2 in consecutive weeks
while making a combined two roster moves, which is either a philosophy or an
accident and at this point does not matter which.

**Grand Theft Votto dropped Trent Grisham and added Bryson Stott** and won eight

**Section 34 and Old Fashioned made no moves.** Section 34 lost the sixth seed by

**PLAYOFF PICTURE**

The sixth seed has changed hands in each of the last two weeks, and it has been
the same two teams both times.

Bip Roberts was seventh and out last Monday, having lost 8–2 to Grand Theft
Votto while Section 34 went 7–2–1 and passed him. This week the reverse
happened: Bip went 7–2–1, Section 34 went 3–6–1, and Section 34 is out by a
game. Bip is 93-87-10; Section 34 is 85-85-20 and 22.5 back. The twenty ties
that carried Section 34 into the field a week ago have stopped carrying him.

The field as it stands, with three weeks to play: Chief Noc-A-Homa, SJ Ballers,
Bad MoFo's, The Chicago Way, Bip Roberts, L-A-Loosh.

Fifth and sixth are effectively a coin flip, and the whole league is watching to
see who reaches Terrapin Station. Bip and Loosh are both at .516 and
both 19.5 back. Section 34 is three games behind them. Nobody else is within
striking distance — Grand Theft Votto is the next closest and would need to make
up six games on Loosh in three weeks while playing the fourth seed on Monday.

At the top, Chief leads SJ Ballers by four for the first bye, down from five a
week ago. Bad MoFo's is fifteen and a half back of first and eleven and a half
back of second, which takes the second bye off the table for everyone but the
two teams already holding it. Both division races are decided: Chief leads the
Colt 45 by eighteen, SJ Ballers leads the Olde English by eleven and a half.

**ON THE TRADE BLOCK**

New feature, and an overdue one. Yahoo has a trading block. It is on the League
tab, in the second row of links, between Transactions and Record Book, filed
under the heading **Trading List**. Nobody opens it, which means every listing
posted to it this season has been read by approximately nobody. From here
forward, if you post it, it runs here.

The complete league trading list, as of Monday morning:

**SJ Ballers** — Available: *Willson Contreras (BOS, 1B)*. Wanted: **K, ERA,
WHIP.** Call it his Deal. Updated Saturday, August 8.

That is not a summary. That is the list. Twelve teams have access to the feature
and one of them has used it, ten days before the deadline, in a season where five
clubs are within four games of a playoff spot and three are sixty games under.

The league office assumes good faith and concedes the tab is hidden. It is also
now a matter of record where the tab is. The form has five fields — players
available, positions available, positions wanted, stats wanted, and a free-text
note — and filling one out takes about as long as reading this sentence.

Some of what is not posted: Section 34 is one game out of the field holding the
number one waiver claim. Grand Theft Votto has won sixteen of its last twenty
categories from eighth place. Bip Roberts and L-A-Loosh are separated by a
tiebreaker for the final spot and have made 132 waiver moves between them, none
of which was a trade. Old Fashioned is 60-120-10.

Every one of those is a team with a stated position and an obvious need.

The block gets printed here every week from now on. Ten days.
